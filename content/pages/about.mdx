Before what I do, it’s important to say who I am. So let’s get personal.

I was born and raised in a small southern Italian coast town; a fact I count
among my blessings. There must be something about growing at the frontier. It
teaches you to grow comfortable with the unknown and unpredictable. Coastal
towns are sanctuaries of duality. They take whatever continuum exists before
them and make it discrete. They are the land of black/white, yin/yang, in/out,
earth/sea, known/unknown, old/new. It’s a tension that becomes fuel if you
know how to burn it.

![A hand-painted signpost on a rocky shore, pointing out to sea towards Croatia, Greece, Albania and Turkey.](/images/about/coast-signpost.jpg)

At the age of 6, my parents took what turned out to be the most impactful
decision of my life: **buying the first family computer**. A Pentium II, with
Windows 95 (second edition of course!), a cathodic monitor screen and zero
internet yet. I was hooked. In a matter of days I must have pressed every
button, opened every app, explored every corner of that virgin digital
territory. Only now I realise how much of a frontier expedition that was.

But why was I totally hooked? The answer was simple: *problem solving*. What I
realised (unconsciously) was that that beeping machine in the dining room was
the shortest feedback loop I had ever witnessed. Every program on that machine
was there to solve a problem. My mission became finding, with methodical
resolution, problems they could solve for me. Later, I discovered one of my
idols calls it “[working on interesting
problems](https://seths.blog/2017/02/interesting-problems/)”.

---

Fast forward a few decades, machines have changed, but my spirit has not.

I’m a founder and engineer. I like being involved in the whole product:
understanding who it’s for, how the business works, what to build, and how it
should feel to use. Taste and technical judgment belong in the same
conversation.

Most of all, I’m an avid autodidact. You can find me geeking out on anything
that catches my attention. From guitar to electronics to philosophy and,
obviously, coding and computer science. Everything is game.

Before software, I studied food science at the University of Gastronomic
Sciences in Pollenzo. I’ve always liked following a question into a field I
know little about and learning enough to make something with it.

AI is giving me the time of my life as a learner and a builder. I’m getting my
hands dirty in things that used to feel out of reach, and building my own
harnesses, agent orchestration and skills to find out how much these tools can
do. Learning how to work with AI is now part of how I approach a new problem.

Writing helps me think through the tradeoffs and explain them to the people I
work with. The public essays are part of that habit.
