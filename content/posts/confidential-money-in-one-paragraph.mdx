Here is the question I keep coming back to. Why is everything you do with money visible to everyone, forever? Public blockchains made value programmable, and as a side effect turned every balance and every payment into a permanent broadcast. The usual fix is to wall the ledger off with permissioned chains and trusted operators, a kind of compliance-friendly opacity that just moves the surveillance somewhere you can't see it.

I think the better answer is encryption that doesn't require you to trust anyone to keep it. With fully homomorphic encryption a contract can add two balances it cannot read, check that a transfer is valid without learning the amount, and settle, all on a public chain with no operator in the middle who could peek.

That's the thesis behind [Raycash](/work/raycash): the privacy of cash with the reach of stablecoins. In practice an encrypted balance is just a handle the contract operates on.

```solidity
// Balances are ciphertext handles, not plaintext uints.
euint64 senderBalance = _balances[msg.sender];
ebool ok = FHE.le(amount, senderBalance);          // amount <= balance, encrypted
_balances[msg.sender] = FHE.sub(senderBalance, amount);
_balances[to]         = FHE.add(_balances[to], amount);
// The chain verified and settled a transfer it never actually saw.
```
