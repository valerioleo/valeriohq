"Privacy with compliance" is the phrase everyone reaches for, and it usually
means a permissioned chain where a small number of parties see everything and
you're asked to trust their restraint.

The better version already exists in the cryptography. You can prove your
balance is above a threshold without revealing it. You can hand an auditor a
view key scoped to one account for one period. You can prove funds didn't come
from a sanctioned address without publishing where they did come from.

So why does none of it ship? Because every one of those flows terminates in a
screen that a normal person has to understand, and we have no vocabulary for
these screens.

Think about what a view key actually is from the user's side. It's a thing you
give someone that lets them see part of your financial life, forever, unless
you can revoke it, and if you can revoke it you now have a second thing to
manage. There is no consumer app in the world that has taught people this
concept. Compare it to "share location until end of day", which billions of
people understand, and which is the same idea with better packaging.

I don't think the gap closes with more cryptography. It closes when someone
designs the disclosure the way Apple designed location permissions: scoped by
default, obvious in the moment, easy to take back, and visible afterwards in a
list of who can see what.

That list, the one showing every disclosure you've ever granted and letting
you pull them back, is the piece I keep sketching and haven't got right yet.
