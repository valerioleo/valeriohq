People ask whether FHE is "fast enough yet" and the honest answer is that the
question is too coarse. Fast enough for what, and measured by whom?

Three numbers matter, and they behave differently.

Gas is the one everyone quotes. An encrypted addition costs orders of
magnitude more than a plain one. That sounds fatal until you notice that a
transfer needs a handful of encrypted operations, not thousands, and that the
comparison people should make is against a chain's base transaction cost, not
against `a + b`.

Latency is the one users feel. Not block time, but the round trip from tapping
send to seeing a state you believe. Anything with an async decryption step
adds a hop, and a payment that resolves in two stages needs an interface that
can honestly represent "sent, not yet final" without making people anxious.

Client-side work is the one that surprised me. Encrypting an input in a phone
browser is not free, and the difference between a flagship phone and a
four-year-old Android is larger than any of the onchain numbers.

The uncomfortable conclusion is that the cryptography is rarely the
bottleneck for the experience. The waiting and the explaining are.
