Digital money is now ubiquitous. All money is dematerialising, going from
atoms to bits. Whatever your net worth, most of it is just an entry in
various ledgers. When you look at it this way, you realise that money… *is
just data*.

Blockchains give us total ownership over that data, with the ability to
update those ledgers without any intermediaries. Any money living under your
private key can be moved only by your private key, sent to whomever you
want, whenever you want.

But if blockchain money is just data, why does it still feel so hard to move
around? What would it take to move money as easily as sending an email?

That’s why we have Raycash.

# Money as a file

Think about how you send a big file today. You don’t ask the recipient which
storage provider they use, or whether they have an account. You upload it,
you get a link, and you send the link wherever the conversation already is.
Whoever opens it, downloads it. WeTransfer turned file delivery into a link.

Blockchain money today works the opposite way. To pay someone you need their
address. They need a wallet before you can send anything. Every transfer is
public. The data is yours, but moving it still means asking the other side
to do the setup first.

# Payment links

At Raycash we are turning a payment into a link. You choose an amount, and
Raycash creates a link that holds the funds. The link goes wherever you
already talk to people: a chat, an email, a QR code on a screen. Whoever
opens it claims the money. If they already use Raycash, it lands in their
balance. If they don’t, the link is where they start: they claim it on the
web, and the funds are theirs from that moment.

It works in both directions. A request link is the mirror image. You ask for
an amount, or leave it open like an invoice, and whoever pays it can do so
with crypto or a card, without an account.

Think of it as a confidential envelope. Everything about the envelope is
private: how much is inside, who sealed it, who opened it. Balances and
transfers are encrypted on chain, so the link is not a public receipt. It
carries money the way a .zip carries files: sealed, portable, and readable
only by the person it reaches.

# Nothing gets stuck

A link that holds money has to answer an obvious question: what if nobody
opens it? Every link has an expiry date, and you can cancel it any time
before it is claimed, so the funds come back to you instead of sitting on
chain forever. Your links live on one screen: open, claimed, cancelled,
expired. You always know where your money is.

Money is data. Data moves as links. Once you see the two together, the rest
follows: sending should feel like sharing, receiving should not require
setup, and none of it should be anyone else’s business.

# Your agents can now speak money.md

The biggest shift in treating money as data is how portable and composable
it becomes. In an era where agents are first-class citizens on the web,
money movement needs to be just one prompt away.

For this reason, Raycash has already built an MCP server where you can
create pay and receive links talking directly to your agents. Upload an
invoice for Acme Co. and send it with a Raycash payment link attached. Or
pay your employees with a bunch of payment links generated on the fly. All
in full confidentiality.

Money as data was always the easy half. Moving it was the hard one, and a
link is what solves it: no address to ask for, no account to open, no public
receipt left behind. Put an agent at either end and the loop closes.
Anything that can hold a conversation can hold a balance, ask to be paid,
and settle up, without a human copying numbers between screens.

That is the part worth getting right. Money should be as portable as the
rest of your data, and a lot more private.
