An experiment in how little lending code you need if you lean on someone
else's settlement layer. Pinkwhale is an NFT lending protocol with no escrow
logic, no settlement logic, and no liquidation engine of its own. It writes
down both possible endings of a loan as marketplace orders, then lets the
clock decide which one becomes real.

The whole thing rests on one realisation: Seaport, OpenSea's settlement
protocol, isn't really a marketplace. It's a general engine for "these items
move this way if and only if those items move that way", with signatures, time
windows and approvals all checked for you. Seen that way a loan is just three
trades: taking it (collateral in, principal out), repaying it (the same trade
backwards), and defaulting (that reverse trade again, priced at zero for the
lender).

So a loan is four ordinary Seaport orders. The lender and borrower sign the
first two in their wallets, and anyone can match them in a single transaction.
Inside that same transaction Pinkwhale mints two more with `seaport.validate`,
which lets a contract be an offerer without ever holding a key: a repayment
order the borrower can fill before expiry, and a default order the lender can
claim for free one second after. The two windows never overlap, so nobody has
to decide later whether a loan defaulted.

The access control rides inside the orders, not in storage. Each resolution
order is `FULL_RESTRICTED`, so Seaport calls back into Pinkwhale's zone before
it will settle, and the zone checks the caller against a hash the signer
committed to. No allowlist, no loan-to-borrower mapping, no storage at all.
Interest comes free too: Seaport interpolates amounts linearly across an
order's window, so "100 now, 110 at expiry" is a rate that accrues by the
second, and Pinkwhale contains no interest code. Collection-wide and
trait-filtered loans fall out of the consideration model for free.

Never shipped as a product. It's a research artifact, unaudited, shared
as-is. The point was to see whether a marketplace protocol could be bent into
a lending one, and how much you get to not build if it can. The interactive
writeup lives at [pinkwhale.valeriohq.com](https://pinkwhale.valeriohq.com).
