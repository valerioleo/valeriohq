I co-founded Raycash to make confidential money useful in everyday life.
Balances and transfers are encrypted using Zama’s fully homomorphic
encryption. The contracts can settle a payment without reading its amount.

I work across the product and lead the engineering practice behind it:
a small team building confidential contracts, a web app and a mobile app.

<RaycashProduct />

# What I own

Product decisions, technical direction, hiring and delivery. I care about
what someone needs from a money app, what makes the business work, and what
we can build well with a small team. That shapes which problems we take on
and what we leave out.

The experience has to make sense before the cryptography does. A deposit
needs a clear state; a payment needs a clear outcome. I work across the
Solidity contracts and the Next.js and React Native apps to connect those
expectations with what the system actually does.

# Tools before headcount

When something keeps taking engineering time, I look for the tool we’re
missing. [Deployoor](/work/deployoor) came from repeatedly wiring contract
deployments into apps and tests. It gives us typed contract objects shared
across the development flow.

[Confidential-primitives](/work/confidential-primitives) collects the FHEVM
patterns we kept reusing. We opened the source so other teams can build on the
same work. The library has been audited by Burrasec.

# Quality lives in the workflow

I favor BDD-first development: describe what a change should do before writing
it, then make that behavior part of the checks. CI gates should block bad
releases automatically, rather than depend on someone remembering a checklist.

AI is part of how we write, review and test code. I’m also building my own
harnesses, agent orchestration and skills, learning how to give these tools
the context and feedback they need. The work still has to meet the release
bar, and the product decisions still need judgment.

# Work you can inspect

The [AsyncWrapper series](/writing/asyncwrapper-anonymous-on-ramp) explains
one of the problems underneath the product: getting public tokens into a
confidential balance, and screening deposits without taking custody. It’s a
useful example of the design tradeoffs I work through.

The [deployment tooling](https://github.com/raycashxyz/deployoor) and
[confidential primitives](https://github.com/raycashxyz/confidential-primitives)
are open source. For the product itself, see [Raycash](https://www.raycash.xyz).
